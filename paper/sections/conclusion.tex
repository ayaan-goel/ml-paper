\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:concl}

In the present study, the robustness of district-level crop-yield models for Gujarat was measured
under missing weather data and geographic distribution shift, separately and together. Holding out
whole districts lowered $R^2$ by 0.08 to 0.13 compared with the random split used in most earlier
studies, and predicting future seasons was harder still, because the upward trend in yields cannot
be extrapolated by tree ensembles. Missing weather caused a smaller loss, which grew with the rate
and was largest when whole variable groups were missing; iterative imputation helped most under
random gaps, but the choice of model mattered more than the choice of imputer, and a random forest
was the best model in almost every setting. Missingness and geographic shift did not compound:
their combined loss was significantly smaller than the sum of their separate losses. Feature
selection retained accuracy with fewer inputs without improving robustness, and location features
helped the linear model only. In practice, a yield model for new districts should be validated with
a district holdout rather than a random split, and a random forest with simple or iterative
imputation can be expected to tolerate moderate gaps in its weather inputs.

Several limitations suggest future work. Only one state, three crops and weather summaries at one
point per district were used, so the findings should be tested in other regions and with weather
aggregated over district boundaries. Soil, irrigation, input use and crop variety were not
available, which limits the accuracy of every model. The missingness was simulated, and datasets
with documented real gaps would be a natural next step. The hyperparameters were fixed, and only
five district groups were used per fold, so the uncertainty of the geographic comparison remains
considerable. District statistics after 2022--23 were not yet released, and the temporal results
could be extended when they become available. Finally, models that handle trends explicitly, such
as detrended targets or hybrid linear--tree models, could combine the strengths of ridge regression
and random forests found here.
